What the lights on the ESP-32 mean

Honeypot mode (default) — the verdict on the last packet received. One of the three stays lit until the next packet.

Red
IO32
 Flagged (score ≥ 50), or a class of neither known nor suspicious — the attacker frames.
Amber
IO17
 Class suspicious and below the flag threshold: something is off, not enough to flag it.
Green
IO16
 Class known and not flagged — a registered ground station behaving normally.
Blue
IO4
 Lit for the whole downlink, while stored records are transmitted to the CTI ground station.

Member mode (M in the serial monitor) — the same three LEDs carry the accept/block verdict a member satellite returns to its OBC, and nothing is stored.

Red BLOCK — the callsign is on the blocklist.
Green ACCEPT. An undecodable frame also lands here: a board that cannot decide fails open, so it never becomes the reason a satellite stops taking commands.
Amber Blocklist hit that the allowlist overrode — accepted, but worth knowing it matched.

Red, amber and green are mutually exclusive: one of them blinks for a second on every packet received, and a new packet cuts the previous blink short. Blue is independent and marks a downlink in progress.
